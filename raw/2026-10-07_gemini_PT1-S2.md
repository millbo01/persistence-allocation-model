**b. Service Lines Recorded on RO Forms**  
The specific service lines and sub-lines recorded on the Revenue Outturn forms are explicitly documented in the accompanying guidance notes. Form RO1 captures "Education services"6. Form RO2 captures "Highways and transport services" and details specific infrastructure maintenance categories6. Its sub-lines mandate the reporting of expenditure for tree and verge maintenance, carriageway sweeping, litter removal, and hazard clearance. Additionally, RO2 captures safety maintenance on principal roads (which formally includes road markings, studs, traffic signals, signal gantries, signs, crossings, illuminated bollards, and communications equipment) as well as routine maintenance on principal roads (encompassing ad-hoc unplanned patching and drainage cleaning)6.  
Form RO3 captures "Social care and public health"6. Children's social care is recorded on this RO3 form, which specifies lines for the "contribution to the health care of individual children," "home care services" for children, and "intensive family interventions" (including interventions linked to the Troubled Families Unit)6. Form RO4 captures "Housing services"6. Form RO5 captures "Cultural, environmental, regulatory and planning services"6. Finally, Form RO6 captures "Protective, central and other services" (also designated in some records as "Trading services"), and records specific items such as administrative costs recoverable from central government6.  
**c. Changes in Line Codes or Definitions**  
Every change in line codes, line names, or definitions between consecutive years in this period, and the specific lines affected, is not stated in the provided documentation.  
**d. Data Revisions and Flagging**  
Published outturn data are subject to revision in later releases. The documentation indicates that revisions to the published datasets are flagged by appending the word "(revised)" directly to the file titles, such as "Revenue outturn service expenditure summary (RSX) 2014 to 2015 (revised)" or "Revenue outturn (RO2) data 2014 to 2015 by local authority (revised)"2. Revisions are also formally demarcated through the scheduled publication lifecycle, moving from "provisional" releases to "final" releases, and in some years via sequentially numbered publications such as a "third release"9.

> 3. WHICH COUNCILS DELIVER WHICH SERVICES

**a. Official Statements of Service Responsibility**  
Official documentation on local government restructuring outlines the functional responsibilities assigned to different tiers of local government. In two-tier areas, county councils are documented as being responsible for services such as Adult Social Care, highways maintenance (including potholes, street lighting, and road safety), and regulatory services and licensing15. Shire district councils in two-tier structures hold responsibility for services such as waste collection and housing15. Unitary authorities are defined as being responsible for the "full range of local government services," consolidating the functions of both county and district tiers17. Any official statement outlining the specific service responsibilities delivered by metropolitan districts and London boroughs is not stated in the provided documentation.

> 5. SERVICE DEMAND SERIES, BY COUNCIL, BY YEAR (2013-14 TO 2020-21)

**a. Adult Social Care** Adult social care demand is tracked via the "Referrals, Assessments and Packages of Care" (RAP) return up to 2013-14, and the "Adult Social Care \- Short and Long Term Support" (SALT) return from 2014-15 onwards. Both datasets are published by NHS Digital20. The exact measures each dataset records, the units of measurement, the specific years available at the council level, definitions of the variables, and the methodological break documented between the RAP and SALT collections are not stated in the provided documentation.  
**b. Children's Social Care** Children's social care statistics are captured in the "Children Looked After by Local Authorities in England (including adoption and care leavers)" dataset, which utilizes the SSDA903 statistical return. This is published by the Department for Education and structured as a longitudinal database holding one record per child23. The children in need census is titled the "Children in Need Survey (2005) (CiN)", also published by the Department for Education23. The exact measures recorded per council per year for these series are not stated.  
**c. Libraries**  
Any official series recording library visits, active borrowers, or the number of libraries per council—including the publisher and any licensing or access restrictions—is not stated in the provided documentation.  
**d. Highways** The Department for Transport publishes the "Road lengths in Great Britain" and "Road traffic statistics" datasets25. The road length statistics capture data measured in miles, broken down by road type (major roads, minor roads, motorways, centrally managed 'A' roads, local authority managed 'A' roads, 'B' roads, and 'C' and 'U' roads) and by rural or urban classification25. The road traffic statistics record traffic volume by local authority and the percentage of road traffic accounted for by each road type25. The exact years available at the local authority level between 2013-14 and 2020-21 are not completely listed, though the years 2017, 2023, and 2024 are referenced25.  
**e. Waste**

# **Documentation of English Local Authority Finance and Related Data**

> 1. REVENUE SPENDING BY SERVICE (England, local authorities)

a. The official returns documenting local authority revenue spending are bifurcated into actual outturn and budget forms. The actual spending returns are officially designated as the Revenue Outturn (RO) suite of forms. This suite encompasses several component forms, including the Revenue outturn summary (RS), Service expenditure summary (RSX), Specific and special revenue grants (RG), Trading account services (TSR), and Subjective analysis return (SAR)1. The budget returns submitted prior to the financial year are designated as the Revenue Account (budget) data returns (RA)4.  
The publisher for these datasets is the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (MHCLG), which was formerly known as the Department for Levelling Up, Housing and Communities (DLUHC)5. Regarding the years available between 2008-09 and 2020-21, the consulted documentation explicitly confirms the availability of outturn and budget forms for the 2012-13, 2016-17, 2017-18, 2019-20, and 2020-21 financial years, alongside subsequent years up to 2026-27; however, the continuous availability of every single discrete year within the 2008-09 to 2020-21 window is not stated8. The publication date of each year's edition is not stated individually for all historical years; however, the scheduled publication cycle dictates that the Revenue Outturn 'first release' (provisional outturn) is published in early Autumn, followed by a second release (final outturn) in December, with further updates occurring depending on the receipt of outstanding data (such as updates published in August or November)6. The Revenue Account budget return deadline is mandated for early April, with publications subsequent to this4. The files are published in OpenDocument Spreadsheet (ODS) and MS Excel Spreadsheet formats and are hosted on the central GOV.UK platform3. (Source: "Local authority revenue expenditure and financing England: 2021 to 2022 final outturn \- Technical notes", Sections: Revisions policy, Data collection6; "General fund revenue account outturn: general guidance notes", Section: Completion of the forms11; "Local authority revenue expenditure and financing: 2026-27 budget, England \- technical notes", Section: Operational context and administrative data collection4).  
b. The specific service categories and their sub-lines are meticulously structured across the RO forms. The structural definitions are outlined below.

| Form Code | Exact Name | Definition |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **RO1** | Education services | Records expenditure on education services. The guidance dictates that this form must explicitly exclude any expenditure relating to academies. If a maintained school converts to an academy during the year, only the financing and expenditure up to the point of conversion are included1. |
| **RO2** | Highways and transport services | Records expenditure on highways and transport. The definitions mandate that expenditure on non-road projects (e.g., refuse disposal) must be excluded and charged to other respective RO forms. Administrative costs recoverable from central government for works on motorways and trunk roads are excluded from RO2 and recorded on the RS form1. |
| **RO3** | Social care and public health | Records expenditure on Adult Social Care and Public Health services. The definition includes work jointly financed with health authorities or clinical commissioning groups (such as the Better Care Fund), which must be recorded at full cost, while partner contributions are logged distinctly as "other income"1. Children's social care is recorded as a separate primary category12. |
| **RO4** | Housing services | Records housing services expenditure, strictly excluding the Housing Revenue Account (HRA). The definition encompasses homelessness prevention and temporary accommodation costs9. |
| **RO5** | Cultural, environmental, regulatory and planning services | Records expenditure deployed for environmental, cultural, and planning statutory and discretionary operations1. |
| **RO6** | Protective, central and other services | Records expenditure for Police services, Fire and rescue services, Central services, unallocated support services, and the net cost of Business Improvement District (BID) ballots1. |

(Source: "General fund revenue account outturn specific guidance notes", Sections: RO1 to RO61; "Local authority revenue expenditure and financing: 2026-27 budget, England", Section: Local authority expenditure14).  
c. The official documentation records multiple specific spending measures, which dictate how local authority finance is aggregated and reported.

| Exact Name | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Gross expenditure** | Total expenditure incurred before the application of any income offsets11. |
| **Income** | Financial inflows specific to a service area, defined to include sales, fees, charges, and contributions from external bodies (such as NHS contributions to pooled budgets)12. |
| **Net current expenditure** | Calculated as expenditure on services net of sales, fees, and charges, as well as other specific income5. |
| **Total Service Expenditure** | The aggregate net figure calculated as total expenditure minus total income that is strictly specific to each service area across all categories14. |
| **Revenue Expenditure** | A broader measure that captures the total budget requirement, extending beyond Total Service Expenditure to include wider financing metrics12. |

The guidance does not explicitly state a recommended measure for comparing services over time; however, the technical notes specify that the statistical releases apply inflation deflators to present figures in "real terms" for multi-year analyses9. (Source: "Local authority revenue expenditure and financing: 2026-27 budget, England", Sections: Definitions, Local authority expenditure14; "General fund revenue account outturn: general guidance notes", Section: Completion of the forms11).  
d. Structural alterations and definition changes to service categories were enacted at multiple junctures over the period. In 2013-14, public health responsibilities were transferred directly to local authorities, resulting in the introduction of a public health ring-fenced grant and the establishment of new dedicated reporting lines on the RO3 form9. Concurrently in 2013-14, the business rates retention scheme was implemented, altering the fundamental structure of core financing reporting, while police authorities were structurally removed from the rates retention scheme and began receiving funding exclusively through a dedicated police grant9. In 2014-15, definitions for education services changed profoundly due to the conversion of multiple local authority-maintained schools into centrally funded academies, removing their expenditure from the RO1 form9. Also in 2014-15, the definition of children's social care changed as "services to young people" were structurally reclassified and moved from education services into children's social care services9. Later, in 2020-21, housing temporary accommodation sub-categories on the RO4 form underwent a comprehensive reclassification. Specifically, historical line codes (40, 42, 44, 45, 46, and 48\) were combined or superseded by new classification codes (83, 84, 85, and 86), while the code for hostels (code 41\) remained structurally unchanged6. (Source: "Local authority revenue expenditure and financing England: 2021 to 2022 final outturn \- Technical notes", Section: Definitions / Data quality6).  
e. The spending data comprehensively covers eligible local authorities in England. By type, this explicitly includes London Boroughs, Metropolitan Districts, Shire Districts, Unitary Authorities, precepting authorities, Police and Crime Commissioners, and Fire authorities4. The documentation does not state which specific service categories each type of authority is responsible for under two-tier administrative arrangements. (Source: "Local authority revenue expenditure and financing: 2026-27 budget, England \- technical notes", Section: Operational context and administrative data collection4).  
f. Documented changes in the specific set of councils over the period (such as reorganisations, mergers, new unitary authorities, or boundary changes) and their corresponding years are not stated within the methodological literature provided.

> 2. FUNDING (THE SCARCITY MEASURE)

a. The official parameters measuring each council's core funding and spending power are systematically defined in the Local Government Finance Settlement documentation.

| Exact Name | Definition | Level | Publisher | Documented Changes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Core Spending Power (CSP)** | A measure of the total resources available to local authorities to fund service delivery. It incorporates Settlement Funding Assessment, council tax, and central government grant streams15. | Per council | MHCLG / DLUHC | The components determining overall spending power were structurally altered in 2013-14 following the introduction of the business rates retention reform9. |
| **Settlement Funding Assessment (SFA)** | Calculated within CSP as the sum of Revenue Support Grant and the Baseline Funding Level, measured before adjustments for authorities with increased Business Rate Retention arrangements15. | Per council | MHCLG / DLUHC | Restructured alongside business rate retention modifications9. |
| **Revenue Support Grant (RSG)** | A core grant structurally calculated by taking the preceding year's amount, adjusting it in line with the change in the Consumer Price Index, and incorporating other rolled-in specific grants15. | Per council | MHCLG / DLUHC | Not stated beyond inflation indexing and grant roll-ins. |
| **Baseline Funding Level (BFL)** | Represents the baseline funding threshold, algorithmically increased to reflect the escalation of the Standard Business Rates Multiplier, accounting for localized shares of rates yields15. | Per council | MHCLG / DLUHC | Not stated. |

The exact years these overarching metrics were available over the 2008-09 to 2020-21 period are not explicitly stated, although their definitions are codified for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 settlement periods. (Source: "Explanatory note on core spending power provisional/final local government finance settlement", Section: Summary / Settlement Funding Assessment9).

| Exact Name | Tied Service | Separation in Returns | Years Existed |

| **Social Care Grant** | Adult and children's social care | Outturn tracking of all specific and special revenue grants is consolidated onto the dedicated RG form1. | Not stated. |

(Source: "Explanatory note on core spending power final local government finance settlement", Sections: Settlement Funding Assessment / Local Authority Better Care Grant15; "General fund revenue account outturn specific guidance notes"1).

The official documentation confirms that the statistical methodologies utilise the Office for National Statistics (ONS) Consumer Price Index (CPI) to index core grants and to structurally express spending amounts in real terms over time15. (Source: "Explanatory note on core spending power final local government finance settlement 2025 to 2026", Section: Settlement Funding Assessment15).

> 3. POPULATION PROJECTIONS (THE EXPECTED FUTURE DEMAND)

a. The official demographic models are formally titled the "Subnational population projections for England"18. They are published by the Office for National Statistics (ONS)19. The documented editions published over the specified period encompass the 2006-based, 2008-based, 2010-based, 2011-based interim, 2012-based, 2014-based, 2016-based, and 2018-based iterations, alongside later 2020-based interim and 2022-based releases18. Regarding publication dates, the 2018-based edition is explicitly documented as being released on 24 March 2020; the exact publication dates for the other historical editions are not stated19. The geographic modelling extends down to Sub Integrated Care Boards, regional boundaries, counties, and local authority levels18. The demographic breakdown is meticulously segmented by single year of age (ranging from 0 to 90+) and sex, as well as aggregated into five-year age groups by sex19. The standard projection horizon models future population size over a 25-year period19. File formats for these datasets are not stated. Past editions definitively remain available, as methodological documentation explicitly details comparisons against previous sets of projections to examine variance against finalized mid-year estimates18. (Source: "Subnational population projections for England: 2018-based", Sections: Summary, Data, Methodology18).  
b. The projections are produced at the geographic level of English local and health authorities18. The methodology indicating whether these strictly mirror the council geographies utilised for spending data, and the precise mathematical handling of local authority boundary changes, is not stated. (Source: "Subnational population projections for England: 2018-based", Section: Summary19).  
c. Additional demographic projections for specific client groups are published alongside the core demographic figures. The ONS produces official household projections, with the 2018-based household projections (designated as Table 406\) published on 29 June 202023. Furthermore, at the local authority level, the ONS provides specialized datasets projecting Old Age Dependency Ratios, State Pension age populations, and working-age populations19. (Source: "Statutory homelessness in England technical note", Section: Data collection23; "Subnational population projections for England: 2018-based", Section: Data19).

> 6. KNOWN LIMITATIONS

The methodological integrity of the datasets is subject to several documented limitations and data-quality caveats. Within the revenue spending returns (RO and RA), official figures are periodically subjected to grossing methodologies or imputation when local authorities issue late or missing returns; as an operational example, the 2021-22 Provisional Outturn aggregates were synthesized based on submissions from only a subset of eligible local authorities, while the Final Outturn required subsequent updates to integrate missing councils4. Furthermore, structural revisions are routinely applied to historical datasets following rigorous pre-defined validation tests by MHCLG and CIPFA, which challenge authorities on inconsistent figures6. Major accounting changes also distort raw figures, such as mandates to hold COVID-19 business rates relief grants within earmarked reserves, which must be algorithmically netted off against total grants and reserve movements37. Comparability warnings are exceptionally prominent across years; data pre-2013-14 cannot be compared reliably to subsequent years due to the statutory transfer of public health responsibilities to councils, the detachment of police funding from the rates retention scheme, and the removal of academy school expenditure from local authority ledgers9. Furthermore, finance statistics for devolved administrations utilise separate structures and cannot be meaningfully compared against English authorities9.  
Regarding service demand and population modelling, the transition to H-CLIC for homelessness statistics suffered from incomplete data migration. Certain local authorities were unable to provide temporary accommodation data through the new system, necessitating the parallel estimation of data via legacy P1E forms, and missing duty end data triggered underreporting warnings23. Subnational population projections are also bounded by rigid definitions; they strictly model the "usually resident" population (excluding short-term migrants and visitors) and, where observed local demographic rates are statistically anomalous, default to national fertility and mortality rates, requiring analytical caution at the granular local level18. (Source: "Local authority revenue expenditure and financing Technical notes", Sections: Data quality, Revisions policy, Definitions4; "Statutory Homelessness Technical Note", Section: Data collection23; "Subnational population projections QMI", Sections: Output information, Quality assurance18).

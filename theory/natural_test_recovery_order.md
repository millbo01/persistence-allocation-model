# Natural test 7: what sets the recovery order? Two rival rules (5 October 2026)

**Status:** theorising (phase 3). The predictions below were committed before searching. Surface level.

## The two rules

After fetal growth restriction, fat comes back before lean tissue (natural_test_fgr.md, FG5). Two explanations were named:

- **Rule L, the type of past load** (Claude, candidate v0.3): after chronic scarcity, a system re-tunes to expect more scarcity and enlarges its reserve first. After acute deprivation, it rebuilds working parts first and reserves last.
- **Rule D, dependency and the next demand** (James, 5 October 2026): the order follows what the system must do next. A dependent infant does not need muscle, because its survival does not depend on its own ability, so reserves come first. A migrating bird needs fuel for the next leg, so fat comes first. A system that must act for itself rebuilds working parts first. This is v0.2's "working parts the next job needs", extended to reserves.

Both rules fit growth-restricted infants (chronic scarcity and dependency). They part company in cases that separate the two conditions:

| Test case | Past load | Next demand | Rule L predicts | Rule D predicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Adults recovering from long-term undernutrition (anorexia nervosa weight restoration) | Chronic | Independent adult who must function | Fat first, overshooting, central | Lean and organ tissue first; fat not favoured |
| B. Animals that must fatten for a coming fast (pre-hibernation or pre-migration refeeding after a short, acute deprivation) | Acute | A fast ahead | Working tissue first, reserves last | Fat first |
| C. Young of precocial species (able to move independently at birth) after growth restriction | Chronic | Must move and keep up from birth | Fat first | Lean first, or at least not fat first |

**Contamination, declared.** Claude knows in outline that weight restoration in anorexia nervosa adds a large share of fat, often central. That favours Rule L in case A and is declared. Cases B and C were not known to Claude.

**What would decide it:**
- **Rule L supported:** fat first in A and C, and working tissue first in B.
- **Rule D supported:** lean first in A and C, and fat first in B.
- **Mixed results:** both rules act, with weights to be found.

## Results (surface level, from search summaries, 5 October 2026)

| Case | Finding | Rule L | Rule D |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A.** Adults after long-term undernutrition (anorexia nervosa) | Fat mass is "completely restored after short-term weight normalization but with a central adiposity phenotype", which normalises after about a year. In early restoration, fat is "preferentially stored in the trunk". In a meta-analysis, fat-free mass and bone density "remained significantly altered" after restoration, although some studies report short-term normalisation of lean mass (PMC6899925 and related) | **Fits:** fat first, central, overshooting in distribution; lean slower or incomplete | **Does not fit:** an independent adult did not put lean first. Contaminated (declared) |
| **C.** Precocial young after growth restriction (lambs, piglets) | Lambs: placental restriction led to catch-up that normalised weight and length "but increasing adiposity in early postnatal life", with reduced skeletal muscle weights and more visceral fat. Piglets: low birth weight brought "greater and earlier onset of fat deposition", with higher body fat and lower muscle weights (Deakin/Adelaide lamb studies; pig studies, via search) | **Fits** | **Does not fit as stated.** Caveat: lambs and piglets walk early but still depend on milk, so case C separates locomotor independence from nutritional dependency only in part |
| **B.** Acute deprivation with a demanding next stage (migrating sparrows) | Food-limited migrating white-throated sparrows lost lean and fat; about 20% of the lean loss was gut and liver. On refeeding with grain, "on average 60% of the increase in body mass [was] lean mass including digestive organs". On fruit they did not regain mass (digestion was the limit) | **Fits:** working tissue, especially intake organs, first | **Does not fit:** the next demand (flight) did not put fat first |
| (Context, not a recovery case) Ground squirrels before hibernation | Mass up 42 to 62%: lean up 17%, fat up seven to eightfold. This is seasonal preparation without prior deprivation | Not applicable | Consistent with next demand driving what is built when slack exists |

## Verdict

**Rule L (the type of past load) fitted all three discriminating cases.** Rule D (dependency and the next demand), as stated, fitted none of them as the main driver of recovery order. Weight: surface level; case A was contaminated, and case C only partly separates the two conditions.

**Where Rule D still has a place.** When a system builds without prior deprivation, it builds for its next demand: pre-hibernation fattening, and the warbler's fuel for its next leg. A combined reading, to test further:
- the type of past load sets the recovery order: acute leads to intake and working parts first; chronic leads to re-tuning and reserves first;
- the intake comes first whenever it was run down (sparrows' digestive organs; the stage 3 rat gut);
- the next demand shapes what is built once recovery is complete, or when there was no deprivation.

**Logged as found:** James's Rule D was named before the test and did not hold as the main driver in these cases. Per James's standing rule, it is recorded as struck in that role; its narrower role (building for a known next demand) remains open.

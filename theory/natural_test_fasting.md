## A contamination point that matters

Refinement C (reserve release falls with level) is the hypothesis the source itself gives for the threshold: "adipose tissue, because of its reduced size, is no longer able to supply fatty acids at an output sufficient" (S1). Claude had read that sentence earlier in this conversation, on 4 October, before writing the refinement. The knee is therefore not an independent derivation, and F2's support is partly built in.

## What the test adds to the model

1. **The threshold switch is real and works as refinement B describes.** In nature, the switch sits at a fixed depletion of the buffer, not at exhaustion, and it changes both metabolism and behaviour together: desertion and food search. Blood loss (sympathetic withdrawal at about 30% loss) and fasting (phase III at threshold adiposity) now show the same structure: a held figure, a buffer drawn down, and an active mode change at a set depletion.
2. **Starting state shapes the whole course.** Initial fatness sets how long phase II lasts and how much protein is spent. A start at or below the threshold skips phase II entirely: the system begins compromised. This is the TQ5 point again, in a second natural system.
3. **Two refinements to consider (for James):**
   - **A plateau:** above a certain reserve size (35% fat), more reserve gives no further benefit, because protein loss is at its floor of about 4% of energy, the obligate share.
   - **Restriction and starvation may differ in recovery order** (S3). If confirmed from the full paper, that would be a natural case where the type of load, not only its size, changes what is restored first.

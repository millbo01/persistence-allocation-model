# Natural test 10: re-tuning after past threats (5 October 2026)

**Status:** theorising (phase 3); testing v0.4's re-tuning outcome and G9 (the cost of re-tuning). The predictions below were committed before searching. Surface level.

**Contamination, declared.** Claude knows in outline:

Most predictions are therefore "consistent at best". The test is whether the re-tuning rules sort them without strain, and whether any of them breaks.

## Results (surface level, from search summaries, 5 October 2026)

| Prediction | Finding | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| RT1. Unpredictable supply leads to larger reserves | Small birds raise fat reserves when food is limited and unpredictable. In starlings, "body weight was higher in the Unpredictable group". Food-insecure birds put on mass "despite eating less": they "increased their energetic efficiency" (body mass kept per unit of food) (Stirling/Newcastle starling studies; PeerJ 11541) | **Consistent.** The re-tuning came through **efficiency**, not more intake: James's "new efficiencies" in a natural case |
| RT2. Re-tuning has a cost | Fat raises predation risk through poorer escape flight and more foraging exposure. A quarter of the 30 commonest small UK bird species lose mass when predation risk rises (mass-dependent predation) | Consistent: the reserve is traded against its cost |
| RT3. A dose window | Four 5-minute coronary occlusions before a 40-minute occlusion cut infarct size by 75% in dogs (Murry, Jennings and Reimer 1986). Brief ischaemia protects; prolonged ischaemia causes the infarct | Consistent (known) |
| RT4. Protection fades | Preconditioning has a first window that starts within 15 minutes and lasts 1 to 3 hours, and a second window from 24 to 72 hours. Muscle (test 8): 3 weeks to 6 months, depending on dose | Consistent |
| RT5. Plant drought memory | Primed plants "close stomata much faster under drought stress" and reopen faster on recovery, with more abscisic acid in guard cells. Stomata were smaller in the second drought cycle. A growth cost when water is plentiful was not found in the sources | Consistent on the benefit; cost not found |
| RT6. Mismatch in people | A meta-analysis of 125 studies found a robust link between perceived food insecurity and high body weight, "restricted to adult women in high-income countries". Animal work suggests unpredictable food raises retained energy and lowers expenditure without more intake (Nettle and colleagues) | **Partly consistent.** The link holds only in one group. Why it is restricted to women in high-income countries is not explained by the model: logged as found |

## What re-tuning adds

- **Re-tuning can work through efficiency.** Food-insecure starlings kept more mass on less food. A system can re-tune by changing how it converts input, not only how much it takes in or stores.
- **Re-tuning has a dose window and a fading memory.** It shows in the heart over hours to days, in muscle over weeks to months, and in plants across drought cycles.
- **The cost is real and traded off:** birds shed fat when predators raise its cost.
- **Logged as found:** the human food-insecurity effect is confined to adult women in high-income countries. The model offers no reason for that restriction.

Weight: surface level; most findings were known in outline.

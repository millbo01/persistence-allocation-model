# Natural test 10: re-tuning after past threats (5 October 2026)

**Status:** theorising (phase 3); testing v0.4's re-tuning outcome and G9 (the cost of re-tuning). The predictions below were committed before searching. Surface level.

**Contamination, declared.** Claude knows in outline:

Most predictions are therefore "consistent at best". The test is whether the re-tuning rules sort them without strain, and whether any of them breaks.

## Predictions (committed before searching)

| No. | Prediction | Model source |
|---|---|---|
| RT1 | **Unpredictable supply leads to larger reserves.** With the same average supply, an unpredictable supply leads to larger reserves than a predictable one. Systems re-tune their reserve to the risk of shortage, not only to its average | Re-tuning; G10 |
| RT2 | **Re-tuning has a cost.** Carrying a larger reserve costs something (predation risk, mobility, metabolic cost), and the reserve is cut where that cost rises | G9 |
| RT3 | **There is a dose window.** A mild or brief first exposure gives protection against a later, larger one. A severe first exposure gives a scar, not protection | Acute-chronic flip; G8 |
| RT4 | **Protection fades.** It lasts for a limited time and fades without re-exposure; longer exposure gives longer protection | Muscle M1 pattern |
| RT5 | **Plants.** Plants that have been through drought respond faster or better to a later drought (primed responses). The cost: reduced growth if water is plentiful | Re-tuning; G9 |
| RT6 | **Mismatch in people.** People who face unpredictable access to food carry more body fat than those with secure access at similar income (the cost of re-tuning in a plentiful setting) | G9 |

# Patterns across the natural tests so far (5 October 2026)

**Status:** theorising (phase 3). Surface-level patterns while the model is built out (James, 5 October 2026). Hard testing comes later. Details are in natural_test_blood_loss.md, natural_test_fasting.md, natural_test_kidney.md and natural_test_honeybee.md.

| Model feature | Blood loss | Fasting | Kidney | Honeybee colony |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Record held flat while load rises | Blood pressure | Function of the protected organs (glucose not checked) | Creatinine, to about 50% nephron loss | Stores and brood intact while adults vanish (collapse disorder) |
| State signals move first | Stroke volume, compensatory reserve | Fat mass, nitrogen excretion | Renal functional reserve, hyperfiltration | Adult numbers; an ever-younger workforce |
| Buffer | Venous volume, constriction of lower-priority beds | Fat | Spare nephrons (redundancy) | Hive bees pulled into foraging; stores |
| Buffer behaves as a stock | Yes: same loss at the break across rates (sheep) | Threshold at a fat share, not a duration | Threshold at a share of nephrons lost | Not examined |
| Break at a set depletion, not exhaustion | About 30% blood loss | About 3 to 9% fat, with 18 to 25% of the initial fat left | About 50% nephrons | Terminal decline once forager death is chronically raised (model) |
| Active switch at the threshold | Sympathetic withdrawal | Phase III, egg desertion, food search | None found | Brood eaten when protein runs short |
| Compounding on survivors | Not examined | Not examined | Hyperfiltration causes sclerosis, which causes more loss | Precocious foraging: younger foragers die sooner, which speeds decline |
| Starting state shapes the course | Heat stress: tolerance down about 70% | Initial fat sets phase II; a lean start skips it | Low nephron number leads to earlier decline | Poor nutrition or pathogen with pesticide act in synergy (bee level) |
| Record recovers, state does not | Not examined | Restriction and starvation recover in opposite orders (weak) | After acute injury, creatinine returns while reserve does not | Not examined |
| Lower priority pays first | Skin, gut and muscle, then kidney; brain only partly protected | Fat, then gut and liver, then muscle | Not applicable | Maintenance and guarding cut; brood care held |
| Control failure at the end | Not examined | Not examined | Not examined | Division of labour breaks down |

**Recurring shape.** In all four systems, a defended figure is held steady while a buffer is drawn down. State markers show the drawdown and the figure does not. The break comes at a set depletion rather than at exhaustion, often through an active switch. The starting state sets how much room there is.

**Gaps to fill in later tests:**
- compounding in blood loss and fasting;
- an active switch in the kidney;
- recovery order in blood loss;
- F7 (whether energy demand moves the threshold).

**Social system (honeybee).** The colony reproduces the institutional turnover dynamic naturally. Lost workers are replaced by unready recruits pulled forward; they fail sooner, the replacement speeds the decline, and division of labour breaks down at the end. The visible record stays intact throughout.

## Plants in drought (natural test 5)

| Feature | Finding |
|---|---|
| Record held flat | Canopy greenness: colour change lags hydraulic failure; healthy-looking trees carry 25 to 31% embolism |
| State signals | Water potential, loss of conductivity, carbohydrate reserves |
| Buffer as a stock | Yes: the same loss of conductivity at death whatever the drought's duration |
| Break at a set depletion | About 80% loss of conductivity (50 to 88% by group) |
| Compounding | Runaway embolism: rapid collapse, few trees seen mid-collapse |
| Lower priority pays first | Leaves and fine roots act as hydraulic fuses |
| Starting state | Drought legacies for 1 to 5 years; repeated droughts worse. **One finding against:** an earlier drought eased stress in a later one in spruce (explanation named, to check) |
| Recovery | Needs new xylem; growth reduced for years |

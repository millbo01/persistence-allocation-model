# Natural test 4: honeybee colony under worker loss. Surface-level check (5 October 2026)

**Status:** theorising (phase 3). Surface level, for patterns. The predictions below were committed before searching.

**Contamination is heavy here.** Claude has already worked with the Khoury et al. 2013 colony model (K1 and K1b, 3 October), the forager-removal reallocation study and the nurse-ratio studies, and has seen Perry et al. named in CONTROL.md. Most predictions are therefore "consistent at best". The value of this test is in checking the shape across a social system.

## Mapping

- **Working parts:** foragers (intake), nurses and hive bees (working parts), brood (the colony's future workforce).
- **Reserve:** stores of honey and pollen.
- **Record:** what a beekeeper routinely sees, which is colony weight, stores and brood area.
- **State signals:** adult worker numbers and age structure, age at first foraging, worker lifespan.
- **Load:** a rise in forager death (pesticide, pathogens, predators).

| No. | Prediction | Contamination |

| H2 | Lower-priority tasks are cut first when workers are lost; brood care is protected | Known (forager-removal study) |

| H7 | Colony weight lags the loss of workers by weeks to months, so weight monitoring warns late | Model output (K1b); data not known to Claude |

## Results (surface level, from search summaries and earlier sources in this conversation, 5 October 2026)

| Prediction | Finding | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| H1. The record holds while the workforce falls | Colony collapse disorder, as defined by its working group: adult bees completely absent, capped brood present, and food stores present and unrobbed. Actively collapsing colonies show "an insufficient number of bees to maintain the amount of brood", a workforce "composed largely of younger adult bees", and the queen present (EDIS factsheets; CCD Working Group, via search) | **Consistent** (known). The brood and stores a beekeeper sees look intact while the adult workforce has gone |
| H2. Lower-priority tasks are cut first | After half the foragers were removed, brood care held at about 21% of workers while maintenance and guarding fell (forager-removal study, 4 October) | Consistent (known) |
| H3. Compounding through precocious foraging | Precocious foragers "completed far fewer foraging trips in their life and had a higher risk of death in their first flights". With chronically raised forager death, "an increasingly younger forager force caused a positive feedback that dramatically accelerated terminal population decline", with "a breakdown in division of labor" (Perry et al. 2015, PNAS 112: 3427 to 3432) | Consistent (known in outline). Division of labour breaking down is the model's control failure: routing stops following priority |
| H4. A compromised start fails sooner | Poor nutrition and a neonicotinoid together cut survival by 50% (synergy); Nosema plus thiamethoxam raised mortality and lowered immunity (synergy studies, via search). Bees respond to nutritional and pathogen stress by foraging early (Perry) | Consistent. The data are at the level of individual bees |
| H5. An active switch: brood eaten when protein runs short | Eggs are eaten first, then young larvae, then older larvae; sealed brood is spared (4 October) | Consistent (known) |
| H6. Workforce back first, stores last | Fed colonies recovered quickly, but those that had eaten their brood could not build winter stores (4 October) | Partly consistent |
| H7. Weight warns late about lost workers | Hive scales are used as early warning mainly for forage and food intake. In collapse disorder, stores stay in the hive while workers vanish, so weight would not show the loss. Researchers point to "the number of active foragers" as the indicator to develop | **Partly consistent** by inference: no study found that measured the lag between worker loss and weight |

**What the honeybee adds.** It is the natural counterpart of the institutional turnover runs:
- foragers lost to raised mortality are replaced by younger bees pulled forward from other work;
- those young bees forage worse and die sooner;
- the replacement itself speeds the decline.

That is the vacancy cascade with fresh, unready recruits (TQ2 and TQ3), and its end point is the breakdown of division of labour (control failure). The visible record (stores and brood) stays intact to the end. The state signal that shows it is the age structure of the workforce, a younger and younger force, which matches "a workforce composed largely of younger adult bees" in collapsing colonies.

Weight: surface level only, and most findings were known in advance. H4 rests on individual-bee studies, not whole colonies.
